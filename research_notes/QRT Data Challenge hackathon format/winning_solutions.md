# Winning and top-ranked solutions to QRT data challenges (ENS Challenge Data + QRT in-person hackathons)

Research date: 23 Nov 2026. "Repo date" means the GitHub created/last-updated date from the GitHub API on 23 Nov 2026. Challenge pages on challengedata.ens.fr were read on 23 Nov 2026. Their leaderboards need a login, so no full leaderboard table was available. Most scores below are self-reported by the participants in their own repositories. Treat them as claims; they are not verified against the official leaderboard.

## Q1. What did top solutions do, challenge by challenge?

### Takeaway
In every QRT challenge, the top solutions won with data understanding and well-built validation. Model choice mattered much less. Examples: recovering hidden time order (electricity), encoding domain knowledge (karyotype → IPSS-R/IPSS-M for AML), declaring the ID column categorical (asset allocation), and thresholding per date (stock returns). They then used small, regularised GBDT or linear models and a simple blend. Edges over the benchmark are tiny in the finance problems (+1 to 2 accuracy points) and large in the non-finance ones (electricity Spearman 0.16 → 0.57; AML C-index ~0.66–0.68 → ~0.72–0.75).

### Cited Findings

#### 1a. Stock Return Prediction (QRT 2020, challenge 23: RET_1..20, VOLUME_1..20, STOCK/SECTOR/INDUSTRY/INDUSTRY_GROUP/SUB_INDUSTRY, DATE)
- Official metric: "the accuracy of the predicted residual stock return sign", which is plain accuracy with no weighting. Benchmark: Random Forest, 500 trees, max depth 8, using the 5 most recent returns and relative volumes, STOCK, and the mean of RET_1 conditioned on SECTOR/DATE. NaNs are filled with 0. "The public score of this benchmark is 51.31%." Winners listed: 1. Romain Poncet, 3. Agnès François & Jessy Idez (no scores shown). — [Challenge 23 page, started 6 Jan 2020](https://challengedata.ens.fr/challenges/23)
- Winning score reported as **0.5287** against benchmark 0.5131. One participant scored 0.5197 (top 15% of 316) using a short window (only the last 5 days, with rows whose last-5 returns were all NaN dropped). Their aggregated features were: average return conditioned on group and sector; weekly MA and std of return and volume; return and volume relative to the sector; momentum; 10-day sector return and volume volatility. Models were a shallow Random Forest (LightGBM's RF mode for fast iteration) with 4-fold CV. — [lbrilh/QRT-StockReturnPrediction README (repo date 2026-01-20)](https://github.com/lbrilh/QRT-StockReturnPrediction); [lbrilh portfolio page](https://lbrilh.github.io/portfolio/project-4)
- 12th of 207 (0.5189) used this code pattern. Weekly (5-day block) mean and std of RET and VOLUME. `groupby(['SECTOR','DATE'])[f'RET_{k}'].transform('mean')` for k=1..4 as "conditional aggregated features". Only RET_1..5 and VOLUME_1..5 plus the engineered features kept, to reduce noise. NaN filled with the column mean. `RandomForestClassifier(n_estimators=500, max_depth=8)`. **KFold(n_splits=4, shuffle=True) applied to the unique DATE values**, so all rows of a date fall in the same fold. Post-processing: `pred = proba; y = sub.groupby('DATE')['pred'].transform(lambda x: x > x.median())`, which thresholds at the per-date median. The notebook says the target "corresponds to the fact that the return is in the top 50% of highest stock returns". Local CV was "around 52", against a public benchmark of 51.31. — [JeremyKulcsarDS/QRT_Challenge_2020_Stock_Return_Prediction notebook (repo date 2026-01-20)](https://github.com/JeremyKulcsarDS/QRT_Challenge_2020_Stock_Return_Prediction)
- Another public notebook reports a GridSearchCV best of **0.5408** using StratifiedKFold random splits (`max_depth=15, min_samples_split=400` on sklearn GradientBoosting). This is far above the reported winning LB score of 0.5287 (see the inference in Q3 about leakage). — [chrxs18/qrt_challenge notebook (repo date 2026-07-20)](https://github.com/chrxs18/qrt_challenge)
- Other 2020 repos describe median imputation of RET/VOLUME, dropping rows with too many missing returns, correlation-based feature pruning, RFE/mutual-information selection, and RF/CatBoost tuning. No scores are given. — [Bilg4me/QRT-2020 (repo date 2025-03-21)](https://github.com/Bilg4me/QRT-2020)
- The QRT 2020 stock dataset was reused as a sponsor challenge at DurHack ("QRT Stock Return Prediction"). A team tried DTW lag-to-sector, latent factor/kNN and transformers ("didn't finish running in time for the hackathon"). — [isaacsbox/QRT-Stock-Return-Prediction (repo date 2026-01-21)](https://github.com/isaacsbox/QRT-Stock-Return-Prediction); [DurHack 2022 Devpost lists a "QRT Stock Prediction Model Challenge"](https://durhack-2022-2.devpost.com/)

#### 1b. "Weighted accuracy" variant: Reconstruction of Liquid Asset Performance (QRT 2021, challenge 44; reused as the QRT APAC Data Challenge 2025)
- Task: predict the sign of the next-day return of 100 liquid assets from 100 illiquid asset returns (RET_0..RET_99, ID_DAY, ID_TARGET, CLASS_LEVEL_1..4). Metric is **|y|-weighted accuracy**, `(1/||y||_1) * Σ |y_i| * 1{ŷ_i = sign(y_i)}`, so large moves count more. Benchmark: for each liquid asset, pick the illiquid asset with the highest absolute correlation, fit β, predict `sign(β * R_k)`. — [Challenge 44 page, started 4 Jan 2021](https://challengedata.ens.fr/challenges/44)
- APAC Data Challenge 2025 entrant: one model per liquid asset (RF/GB/XGB/CatBoost, Optuna 20 trials, choose the best per asset by F1). No score reported. — [lhurr/qrt-data-challenge (repo date 2025-09-06)](https://github.com/lhurr/qrt-data-challenge)
- Another entrant trained a multi-head MLP with **weighted BCE loss** (weights = |return|) to match the metric. No score reported. — [dndcreator/QRT-Data-Challenge-2025 (repo date 2026-02-09)](https://github.com/dndcreator/QRT-Data-Challenge-2025)

#### 1c. Learning factors for stock returns / Stiefel manifold (QRT 2022, challenge 72)
- Output is A (250×10, orthonormal columns) and β (10). The metric is the mean cosine similarity between predicted and realised returns over t=250..753, and it is set to -1 if `|<A_i,A_j> − δ_ij| > 1e-6`. Benchmark: 1000 random Gaussian matrices → Gram-Schmidt → least-squares β, keep the best. Winners listed: 1. New_try, 2. lp505, 3. Nicolas Raoult (no scores shown). — [Challenge 72 page, started 5 Jan 2022](https://challengedata.ens.fr/challenges/72)
- The random-Stiefel benchmark reached **≈0.046** (training metric, 1000 iterations). Three torch approaches each reached a local maximum of **≈0.149**: (1) exponential-map parametrisation of O(D) with Adam, in 200 epochs; (2) a penalty ‖AᵀA − I‖² with SGD and a LambdaLR schedule, in 2000 epochs; (3) Lagrange multipliers with Adam, in 2000 epochs. — [mrmrob003/qrt-data-challenge-2022 (repo date 2025-04-28)](https://github.com/mrmrob003/qrt-data-challenge-2022)
- One solution used torch as a constrained optimiser (no deep learning, since the model must stay linear). It was ranked **2nd on the public LB but 78th on the private LB**. — [PirashanthR/QRT-Data-Challenge-2022 (repo date 2026-04-15)](https://github.com/PirashanthR/QRT-Data-Challenge-2022)

#### 1d. Electricity prices: "Can you explain the price of electricity?" (QRT 2023, challenge 97; Spearman)
- Benchmark: linear regression with NaN → 0 and COUNTRY dropped (one model for FR and DE). **Benchmark 15.86%** Spearman. Winners: 1. Mohamed Ali Ben Amara, 2. "mb", 3. Amine Abdelkader (no scores shown). — [Challenge 97 page, started 6 Jan 2023](https://challengedata.ens.fr/challenges/97)
- Rank **6/1157**, public LB **0.5658**, CV 0.5716 ± 0.0050 (day-grouped 5-fold, 15–20 seeds). Each step was kept only if it beat the previous one on nearly every seed, on identical folds. Step-by-step CV: separate model per country 0.236 → rank-transform target 0.290 → **recover day ordering from `ID` (DAY_ID is shuffled, ID is not) 0.524** → 5- and 20-day cumulative commodity returns for France 0.546 → neighbouring days' targets 0.552 → shrink France's prediction spread 0.558 → +LightGBM 0.569 → +pooled CatBoost 0.572. — [ryan-racer/QRT-Electricity-Forecasting (repo date 2026-09-18)](https://github.com/ryan-racer/QRT-Electricity-Forecasting)
- That solution's final model: RidgeCV per country (α by LOO over `logspace(-2,4,40)`); LightGBM per country (`num_leaves=4, n_estimators=400, lr=0.05, min_child_samples=20, lambda=1, subsample=0.8, colsample=0.7`); CatBoost pooled with a country flag (`depth=6, iterations=400, lr=0.05, l2=6`). All fit a **rank-transformed target** with median imputation. Predictions are z-scored and blended as `0.8*(0.7*ridge + 0.3*lgbm) + 0.2*catboost`, then **France's predictions are shrunk halfway toward their mean** so that the more predictable German rows take the extremes of the pooled ranking. Normalising both countries onto a common scale "loses 0.048". France used only 3 base columns, because a permutation test passed only 3 of 29 for FR (9 for DE). Trailing windows use `shift(1)`. — [ryan-racer/QRT-Electricity-Forecasting](https://github.com/ryan-racer/QRT-Electricity-Forecasting)
- Same source: "Under the original 29 features every flexible model lost to ridge. Once the time features existed, the identical LightGBM config flipped from −0.013 to +0.023 against it. Signal-to-noise, not model class." It also notes that 1% of days carry 31% of squared-error weight, so least squares works against a rank metric. — [ryan-racer/QRT-Electricity-Forecasting](https://github.com/ryan-racer/QRT-Electricity-Forecasting)
- A more typical result: 6 models compared, best LightGBM at **27.49% Spearman, 77/866**. — [talhajamal11/QRT---Electricity-Prices---2023 (repo date 2025-05-31)](https://github.com/talhajamal11/QRT---Electricity-Prices---2023)

#### 1e. Football: "Can you guess the winner?" (QRT 2024, challenge 143; 3-class accuracy)
- Benchmarks: always home win ≈44%; gradient boosting on team data only, predicting whether the away team wins, ≈47.5% (the leaderboard benchmark). — [Challenge 143 page, started 10 Jan 2024](https://challengedata.ens.fr/challenges/143)
- **4th of 800+**: median imputation, player stats aggregated by team and **by position**, home/away context features, XGBoost/CatBoost/TabNet tuned with Hyperopt, combined with sklearn `StackingClassifier`, Stratified KFold. — [hugopuybareau/QRT_DATA_CHALLENGE (repo date 2026-05-06)](https://github.com/hugopuybareau/QRT_DATA_CHALLENGE)
- QRT × Télécom Business Finance edition: **1st public / 2nd private, accuracy 0.4861 vs top private 0.4875**. Ensemble of SVM + XGBoost + CatBoost + MLP. Rows with missing values were dropped, with a train/validation split. — [ayoub-mg/QRT-Football-Challenge (repo date 2024-10-05)](https://github.com/ayoub-mg/QRT-Football-Challenge)
- Stack of LightGBM + XGBoost + CatBoost, about **2,800 engineered variables**, feature selection inside each fold, and an auxiliary "predicted goal difference" feature. Online **0.4913** (CV 0.4935–0.4956). "0.49–0.50 is the top of the leaderboard." TabICL (a tabular foundation model, no tuning) matched it (CV 0.4952) and agreed on 92% of predictions. Adversarial validation separates train from test with **AUC 0.98**, "which is why cross-validation gains do not carry over". All variants fell within 0.004 online, which is about the LB uncertainty of ±0.003 on 25,000 matches. — [louisrodembourg/qrt-football-prediction (repo date 2026-11-18)](https://github.com/louisrodembourg/qrt-football-prediction)
- XGBRegressor on **goal difference** plus XGBClassifier, position-based aggregates (mean/sum/max/std) over a "simulated starting lineup" (players averaging 45+ min), Elastic-Net feature selection, Optuna. Reports **0.4899**. PCA, autoencoders and ridge/lasso were tried and "experienced a significant drop in performance particularly with PCA". — [ms2621/QRT_Football (repo date 2025-01-12)](https://github.com/ms2621/QRT_Football)
- Rank 112 of 900+ (no method details in the README). — [atbrt/qrt_challenge (repo date 2025-07-27)](https://github.com/atbrt/qrt_challenge)

#### 1f. AML / myeloid leukemia overall survival (QRT × Gustave Roussy, challenge 162; IPCW C-index, τ = 7 years)
- Metric: scikit-survival IPCW C-index truncated at 7 years. Submit `ID, risk_score`; only the ordering matters. Benchmarks: LightGBM on clinical data ignoring censoring, and **Cox PH on clinical data plus limited mutation info (the ranking benchmark)**. Winners: 1. Arthur De Rouck & Ruben Barata, 2. Octave Cerclé, 3. Ryan Mathieu (no scores shown). The page says the challenge "Started on Jan. 13, 2026", but the 2025 repos below already link challenge 162, so the start date is inconsistent (possibly a re-opened season). — [Challenge 162 page](https://challengedata.ens.fr/challenges/162)
- Best-documented solution: **all 1,193 test patients come from one hospital (`KYW`) that never appears in training** (3,173 labelled train patients from 23 centres). Validation was therefore leave-centres-out (5 folds of whole hospitals × 3 random assignments = 15 folds). Leave-centres-out C-index results: IPSS-R alone 0.6533; organisers' Cox benchmark 0.6847; elastic-net Cox on 224 features 0.7058; re-implemented **IPSS-M alone 0.7086**; best single model, **multi-horizon IPCW XGBoost 0.7226**; **rank-average ensemble of 10 members 0.7238**. The ensemble beat every baseline on all 15 folds and its best member on 11/15. — [lucaspaulien/qrt-leukemia-survival (repo date 2026-10-20)](https://github.com/lucaspaulien/qrt-leukemia-survival)
- Its features: an ISCN karyotype parser (splits clones on `/`, handles `idem`, about 50 counts and flags: number of abnormalities, monosomal and complex karyotype ≥3/≥4, −5/del(5q), −7/del(7q), +8, del(20q), −Y, 17p, inv(3), 11q23, t(8;21), inv(16), t(15;17), t(9;22)) mapped to the 5 IPSS-R cytogenetic groups. Mutation burden; VAF summaries normalised because some centres report VAF in percent; flag and max VAF for about 50 myeloid genes; multi-hit counts (TP53, TET2, ASXL1); pathway burdens; FLT3-ITD; IPSS-R raw score and IPSS-M linear predictor as features. Model families chosen to "disagree in how they treat time": XGBoost Cox loss, sksurv GB Cox, elastic-net Cox, XGBoost AFT (log-normal), random survival forest, extra survival trees, and discrete-time multi-horizon classifiers (one binary "dead by year h" model per h ∈ {0.5,1,2,3,5,7}) with IPCW. — [lucaspaulien/qrt-leukemia-survival](https://github.com/lucaspaulien/qrt-leukemia-survival)
- A submission scored **0.7541 IPCW**: **stacked ensemble with a CoxPH meta-model** over linear and tree survival models. Per-model submission scores: Cox PH baseline 0.6563; CoxNet (elastic-net) 0.7441; component-wise gradient boosting 0.7493; GBSA (full data) 0.7462. Preprocessing: median imputation **per centre**, 1st/99th percentile capping, log transforms, ISCN parsing to an IPSS-R score, top-23 genes with VAF max/mean, one-hot mutation effects. Top drivers: HB (protective), vaf_TP53, log BM_BLAST, log PLT, n_mutations. — [coquetl/Myeloid-Leukemia-Prediction (repo date 2026-03-10)](https://github.com/coquetl/Myeloid-Leukemia-Prediction)
- A progression from another team (CV): benchmark-like without censoring 0.6665 → feature engineering 0.7075 → CoxPH/RSF 0.7160 → DeepSurv 0.7113 (deep learning did not beat the classical models). "CatBoost outperform[ed] other boosting algorithms". — [MaxCoppa/Myeloid-Leukemia-Survival-Prediction (repo date 2025-12-04)](https://github.com/MaxCoppa/Myeloid-Leukemia-Survival-Prediction)
- Data size conflict: 3,323 training patients per MaxCoppa and coquetl vs "3,173 labelled" per lucaspaulien. The gap is probably patients with missing labels (inference).

#### 1g. Asset Allocation Performance forecasting (QRT Grand Data Challenge, Oct 2025, in person; then ENS challenge 167 from Jan 2026)
- ENS version columns: TS, ALLOCATION, RET_1..20, SIGNED_VOLUME_1..20, MEDIAN_DAILY_TURNOVER, GROUP. Target is the sign of the next-day return; metric is accuracy. **Benchmark: LightGBM + Ridge with historical performance and volatility features, public LB 0.5079.** — [Challenge 167 page, started 22 Jan 2026](https://challengedata.ens.fr/challenges/167)
- Always-long baseline 0.5072, so "the entire edge available on this problem is about 1.3 points". Best: **60/40 rank blend of LightGBM + SeqTabNet (1D-CNN + tabular MLP), 0.5215 on test-like days / 0.5272 on all days**. Biggest single gain: **declaring `ALLOCATION` as a categorical feature** (+0.0044, t=+4.13, 11/12 folds), versus parsing the integer out of "ALLOCATION_123". Permutation importance: ALLOCATION 0.0116, `reversal_1_vs_5` 0.0049, `mkt_n_allocations` (number of allocations on that date) 0.0017, `turnover_per_volume` 0.0013, `ALLOCATION_hit_rate` 0.0009. — [hobbit-joufflu/Data-Challenge-2026 README + RESULTS.md (repo dates 2026-02-15 / 2026-08-12)](https://github.com/hobbit-joufflu/Data-Challenge-2026)
- Holdout of 504 dates: always-up 50.6%, logistic 52.3%, **"equal3" ensemble 52.4%**. Equal3 is the average of probabilities from ridge-logistic + ridge on `asinh(target)` + LightGBM with frozen hyperparameters, thresholded at 0.5 with no tuned weights, each trained on three random 80% subsets of dates. Date-grouped 5-fold on 527,073 rows: logistic 52.49%, ridge-asinh 52.49%, LightGBM 52.53%, equal3 52.65% (+0.16 pt vs logistic, CI [+0.06, +0.26]). Features fitted inside each fold: median-imputed, standardised raw returns and volumes with missing indicators; one-hot GROUP and ALLOCATION; **18 cross-sectional features (rank, distance to median, robust z-score within the date)**. — [martindelamare/qrt-asset-allocation-forecasting (repo date 2026-09-22)](https://github.com/martindelamare/qrt-asset-allocation-forecasting)
- Grand Data Challenge 2025 (in person): 104 teams, 100+ students from CentraleSupélec, Polytechnique, ENSAE, Télécom, Mines and Ponts, CV-selected. A team ranked 50/104 after 100+ hours. They tried Ridge/ElasticNet/Huber, XGBoost/LightGBM, LSTM and stacking. Lesson: "impressive validation scores that didn't translate to the leaderboard" because of leakage in preprocessing. Training set 180,245 rows in the event version. — [malerbe/QRT_challenge (repo date 2026-09-13)](https://github.com/malerbe/QRT_challenge)
- Same event: "8-day hackathon", about 200 students from X, CS, ENSAE and Mines. 31st place with LogisticRegression as the kept model. RF/XGB/LGBM, **per-allocation models** and blending were "removed from the notebook because less performant here". — [iliasbendali/QRT-Grand-Data-Challenge (repo date 2025-10-25)](https://github.com/iliasbendali/QRT-Grand-Data-Challenge)
- Ranked **13/200** with LightGBM as the main model plus XGBoost and Ridge/LinearRegression baselines. Features: 20-day lags, rolling means, dynamic ratios, normalised gaps; an autoencoder for dimensionality reduction; target encoding; Optuna; time-period CV; SHAP. — [leclercrafael/QRT-Asset-Allocation-Performance-forecasting (repo date 2026-09-26)](https://github.com/leclercrafael/QRT-Asset-Allocation-Performance-forecasting)
- A post-event pipeline "would have placed us at position 3 out of more than 100 teams". The method focuses on evaluation: **100 repeated random splits of TS blocks** (grouped by TS) to treat performance "as a distribution, not a point estimate". — [pradosth0/QRT_Asset_Allocation_Performance_forecasting_2025 (repo created 2025-10-21)](https://github.com/pradosth0/QRT_Asset_Allocation_Performance_forecasting_2025)
- Another participant: benchmark-style features `AVERAGE_PERF_{3,5,10,15,20}`, group averages `ALLOCATIONS_AVERAGE_PERF_*` and rolling std (about 45 features). Ridge α=1, plus LightGBM `max_depth=3`, 500 rounds, lr 0.01, objective MSE, 8-fold CV → **52.22% ± 0.20%**. Self-reported tuned RF (OOB) ~53–54%, an OOB estimate not confirmed on the LB. Top features: GROUP, CUSUM filters, fractionally differentiated returns, group averages. Correlation-cluster pruning cut 140 features to about 40 "with no accuracy loss". — [lflsphere/QRT-Asset-Allocation-Perf (repo date 2026-09-25)](https://github.com/lflsphere/QRT-Asset-Allocation-Perf)

### Inferences
- (Inference) QRT reuses its datasets. The 2020 stock dataset showed up at DurHack, the 2024 football dataset at the QRT × TBF in-person event (6 June 2024), the 2021 liquid-asset dataset as the "APAC Data Challenge 2025", and the Oct 2025 Grand Data Challenge became ENS challenge 167 in Jan 2026. The ETH problem may well be a variant of one of these. Prepared pipelines for (a) cross-sectional return-sign classification with RET_1..20/VOLUME_1..20 and group IDs, (b) a tabular multi-class sports outcome task, and (c) survival or rank-metric regression cover most of the history.
- (Inference) In the finance problems a realistic win is +1 to 2 accuracy points over the benchmark: 2020 winner 0.5287 vs 0.5131; Grand Data Challenge about 0.52 vs 0.5079. In the non-finance problems most of the gain comes from domain or structure features.

## Q2. Which feature transformations mattered most?

### Takeaway
The features that paid off were: cross-sectional (per-date) aggregates, ranks and robust z-scores; group-level averages (SECTOR×DATE, GROUP, allocation hit rate); short-window aggregates (last 5 days, weekly mean/std, reversal RET_1 vs RET_5); native categorical treatment of entity IDs; domain-knowledge encodings; and rank-transforming the target for rank metrics. Large raw lag sets, PCA and autoencoders, and heavy target encoding mostly added noise.

### Cited Findings
- 2020 stock benchmark and top-15% solutions: the key feature is the conditional mean of RET_k by **(SECTOR, DATE)** via `groupby(...).transform('mean')`. They keep only the last 5 days of RET/VOLUME "to reduce noise", plus weekly (5-day block) mean/std of RET and VOLUME, and return/volume relative to the sector. — [JeremyKulcsarDS notebook](https://github.com/JeremyKulcsarDS/QRT_Challenge_2020_Stock_Return_Prediction); [lbrilh README](https://github.com/lbrilh/QRT-StockReturnPrediction); [Challenge 23 benchmark description](https://challengedata.ens.fr/challenges/23)
- Missing values in 2020: about 15% of VOLUME columns and about 1% of RET columns are NaN. The benchmark fills with 0; others use the column mean or median, or drop rows whose recent returns are all NaN. — [JeremyKulcsarDS notebook](https://github.com/JeremyKulcsarDS/QRT_Challenge_2020_Stock_Return_Prediction); [lbrilh README](https://github.com/lbrilh/QRT-StockReturnPrediction); [Bilg4me README](https://github.com/Bilg4me/QRT-2020)
- Asset allocation: **18 cross-sectional features (rank, distance to median, robust z-score within the date)**, missing-value indicators, and every statistic fitted inside each fold. — [martindelamare](https://github.com/martindelamare/qrt-asset-allocation-forecasting)
- Asset allocation: `ALLOCATION` as **categorical** gave +0.0044. Target encoding of ALLOCATION "adds nothing" on top of the categorical declaration; it is useful only for the NN, which has no categorical support. Top non-ID features: `reversal_1_vs_5`, number of allocations on the date, turnover per volume, allocation hit rate. Not significant: GROUP-relative ranks, `VOL_1` flow, Hurst exponent, Optuna tuning (+0.0005, t=0.86). — [hobbit-joufflu RESULTS.md](https://github.com/hobbit-joufflu/Data-Challenge-2026/blob/main/RESULTS.md)
- Asset allocation, what did NOT work: **de-meaning the target by day** lost −0.0034 to −0.0043 (t ≈ −3.5), even though 93.4% of target variance is intra-day, because it "throws away the market drift" that the always-long baseline captures. Down-weighting sparse days lost −0.0013. Removing negative-permutation-importance features hurt (−0.0010). Removing |y| sample weighting was neutral. — [hobbit-joufflu RESULTS.md](https://github.com/hobbit-joufflu/Data-Challenge-2026/blob/main/RESULTS.md)
- Asset allocation, bugs that cost score: **imputing before building features** made the NaN-safe aggregations inert; a median/IQR z-score blew up to ~1e9 when a day's IQR was 0; Optuna's `num_leaves` bound was set as `2*max_depth-1` instead of `2**max_depth-1`; a stacking layer used hard 0/1 in-fold meta-features and scored below its base models; `import torch` before LightGBM caused a SIGSEGV (OpenMP conflict). — [hobbit-joufflu RESULTS.md](https://github.com/hobbit-joufflu/Data-Challenge-2026/blob/main/RESULTS.md)
- Asset allocation: GROUP (regime) and group-average performance were the strongest features. Raw return lags "contribute least". — [lflsphere](https://github.com/lflsphere/QRT-Asset-Allocation-Perf)
- Electricity: rank-transforming the target was worth +0.04 "for free". Day-over-day differences and deviations from a 7-day trailing mean (using `shift(1)`) became possible after recovering calendar order from `ID`. Also: 5- and 20-day cumulative commodity returns for France ("France is priced off levels, Germany off changes"), and level/vol/spread of the 3 nearest training days' targets. FR and DE rows for a day are bit-identical except COUNTRY and target, and the two targets correlate at only 0.12, so model per country. — [ryan-racer](https://github.com/ryan-racer/QRT-Electricity-Forecasting)
- Football: aggregate player stats **by position** and by team; home-minus-away or win-rate features (e.g. `HOME_WR = GAME_WON_season_average + 0.5*GAME_DRAW_season_average`); drop features correlated above 0.8; an auxiliary goal-difference regression used as a feature or model. — [hugopuybareau](https://github.com/hugopuybareau/QRT_DATA_CHALLENGE); [ms2621](https://github.com/ms2621/QRT_Football); [louisrodembourg](https://github.com/louisrodembourg/qrt-football-prediction); [rwitt74/QRT_hackathon notebook (repo date 2024-09-26)](https://github.com/rwitt74/QRT_hackathon)
- AML: domain encodings (IPSS-R from the parsed karyotype; IPSS-M linear predictor; TP53 multi-hit; VAF normalisation across centres) drove most of the gain. IPSS-M alone scored 0.7086, versus 0.7238 for the full ensemble. Per-centre median imputation and log transforms of blood counts were also used. — [lucaspaulien](https://github.com/lucaspaulien/qrt-leukemia-survival); [coquetl](https://github.com/coquetl/Myeloid-Leukemia-Prediction)
- Football: PCA caused "a significant drop in performance". — [ms2621](https://github.com/ms2621/QRT_Football)

### Inferences
- (Inference) The most reusable features for a QRT return-sign problem: (1) `groupby(DATE)` rank and robust z-score of RET_1..5, VOLUME_1..5 and short-window sums; (2) `groupby([DATE, SECTOR or GROUP])` mean of RET_k and the stock's deviation from it; (3) 5/10/20-day mean, std and sum of RET; reversal `RET_1 − mean(RET_1..5)`; (4) count of entities per date; (5) entity ID as a LightGBM categorical. Build features **before** imputing and let LightGBM handle NaN natively.
- (Inference) For rank-based metrics (Spearman, C-index), rank-transform the target (or use a ranking or survival loss) and z-score each model's output before blending.

### Gaps
- No source quantified the separate value of cross-sectional normalisation per date in the 2020 stock challenge. The evidence is from the 2025-26 allocation challenge.
- No winner write-up on the |y|-weighted-accuracy metric (2021) quantified the gain from |y| sample weights. In the allocation challenge, |y| weighting was neutral ([hobbit-joufflu](https://github.com/hobbit-joufflu/Data-Challenge-2026/blob/main/RESULTS.md)). lflsphere claims vol-scaled sample weights were among the "most impactful" changes but gives no number.

## Q3. Which validation schemes avoided leakage, how well did local CV track the leaderboard, and were there shake-ups?

### Takeaway
Group k-fold by date was standard and necessary for all panel and finance problems. Leave-group-out by the shift variable (hospital centre) was decisive for AML. Even with good CV, public leaderboards are noisy (±0.3 to 1.4 pt), and QRT challenges have had dramatic public-to-private shake-ups. Top finishers chose submissions from CV, not public LB, and stopped tuning once differences fell within LB noise.

### Cited Findings
- The QRT 2020 benchmark itself splits folds over **unique DATE values** (`KFold(4, shuffle=True).split(train_dates)` then `isin`). Local CV was about 0.52 against a public benchmark of 0.5131. — [JeremyKulcsarDS notebook (benchmark code reproduced)](https://github.com/JeremyKulcsarDS/QRT_Challenge_2020_Stock_Return_Prediction)
- Random StratifiedKFold on the 2020 data gave a CV best of 0.5408, far above the reported winning LB of 0.5287. — [chrxs18 notebook](https://github.com/chrxs18/qrt_challenge); [lbrilh README](https://github.com/lbrilh/QRT-StockReturnPrediction)
- Asset allocation: "Validation grouped by date (TS). The allocations of a given date share a common market factor. Splitting a date between train and validation would leak that factor." Leakage avoided: `ROW_ID` is never a feature (a 1-NN on ROW_ID scores 100% in-sample). Dates can be partly re-ordered because "a date's target reappears as the next date's RET_1 (88% sign agreement)"; the author deliberately excluded this. Leaderboard noise is about ±1.4 pt, against model differences of 0.1–0.2 pt. — [martindelamare](https://github.com/martindelamare/qrt-asset-allocation-forecasting)
- Asset allocation: 12-fold GroupKFold on TS with paired comparisons, a day-clustered SE and bootstrap over days. **Measuring CV only on "test-like" days (≥270 allocations) predicted the private score within 0.0018**, whereas all-days CV was optimistic by 0.008, because sparse train days are "easier". Public→private shift on one submission: 0.5233 → 0.5158 (−0.0075). Public LB SE about ±0.005 with about 120 test days. — [hobbit-joufflu RESULTS.md](https://github.com/hobbit-joufflu/Data-Challenge-2026/blob/main/RESULTS.md)
- Asset allocation: three CV schemes run side by side: KFold over date groups, purged+embargoed CV (embargo 0/5/20 days; "confirmed no meaningful leakage"), and GroupKFold by ALLOCATION. — [lflsphere](https://github.com/lflsphere/QRT-Asset-Allocation-Perf)
- Grand Data Challenge 2025 lesson: "impressive validation scores that didn't translate to the leaderboard… subtle data leakage issues in our preprocessing pipeline… feature engineering can inadvertently introduce future information". — [malerbe](https://github.com/malerbe/QRT_challenge)
- In-person kit warning: in another team's ExtraTrees pipeline, TS target encoding was computed before the CV split, so "its reported mean accuracy should not be treated" as reliable. — [ferdinandfd/QRT-Hackathon (repo date 2026-09-26)](https://github.com/ferdinandfd/QRT-Hackathon)
- Electricity: day-grouped 5-fold, 15–20 seeds, identical folds across variants. CV 0.5716 ± 0.005 → public 0.5658, so CV tracked the LB well. Neighbour-target features draw only on training targets, and a day is never its own neighbour. — [ryan-racer](https://github.com/ryan-racer/QRT-Electricity-Forecasting)
- AML: leave-centres-out CV (whole hospitals) because the test set is a single unseen centre. Random 5-fold gives 0.7246 ± 0.0033 vs leave-centres-out 0.7220 ± 0.0077: similar mean, twice the uncertainty. "Which hospitals you hold out matters more than which model you use" (fold range 0.690–0.792). — [lucaspaulien](https://github.com/lucaspaulien/qrt-leukemia-survival)
- Football: adversarial validation AUC 0.98 (train vs test differ). CV gains did not carry over. Ensemble weights chosen with nested CV. A "proxy validation" set was built from test-like training rows. The author "stopped optimising against the leaderboard so as not to overfit it". — [louisrodembourg](https://github.com/louisrodembourg/qrt-football-prediction)
- **Shake-up, Learning factors 2022:** "ranked 2nd in the public leaderboard but only 78th on the private… 1st public LB → 81st private LB, 3rd public → 74th private / 99th public → 1st private, 29th public → 2nd private". — [PirashanthR](https://github.com/PirashanthR/QRT-Data-Challenge-2022)
- **Football (TBF edition):** 1st public → 2nd private (0.4861 vs top 0.4875). — [ayoub-mg](https://github.com/ayoub-mg/QRT-Football-Challenge)

### Inferences
- (Inference) For a 4-hour event, default to `GroupKFold(n_splits=5, groups=DATE)` (or the ID that defines the cross-section). Fit all imputers, encoders and target encoders inside folds. If the test set differs in a structural way (fewer or more entities per date, an unseen hospital or season), score CV on the test-like subset or with leave-group-out. Run a quick adversarial-validation check (train vs test classifier AUC) in the first 30 minutes.
- (Inference) With about 0.5% LB noise on accuracy problems, do not chase public-LB improvements under about 0.5 pt. Choose the final submission from multi-seed grouped CV.
- (Inference) The "recover hidden order from ID" trick (electricity) and "target reappears as next date's RET_1" (allocation) show that QRT anonymisation can leak structure. Ask organisers whether exploiting it is allowed. One top solution used it; another explicitly refused it as leakage.

### Gaps
- No source reports the extent of public→private shake-up in the 2020 stock or 2023 electricity challenges.
- I found no purged-CV comparison with numbers showing a CV vs LB gap for a QRT problem. lflsphere reports only "no meaningful leakage".

## Q4. Which models won, and how much did ensembling/stacking help?

### Takeaway
Gradient-boosted trees (LightGBM, CatBoost, XGBoost) with shallow trees and strong regularisation are the workhorse. On very noisy finance targets, (ridge-)logistic regression is just as good. Blending 2–3 diverse models (linear + GBDT ± NN or CatBoost) with equal weights or rank averaging gives small but consistent gains: about +0.1 to 0.2 pt accuracy, +0.011 to 0.014 Spearman (adding LightGBM, then CatBoost, to ridge), and +0.001 to 0.005 C-index (+0.0012 in leave-centres-out CV for a rank average; +0.0048 for a CoxPH-meta stack, measured on submission scores, not CV). Elaborate stacking, deep nets and per-entity models rarely paid off.

### Cited Findings
- Asset allocation: LightGBM 52.53% vs logistic 52.49% (difference not significant). The equal-weight average of logistic + ridge-asinh + LightGBM gave **+0.16 pt** (significant). "Most of the ensemble's gain comes from dates with few allocations." — [martindelamare](https://github.com/martindelamare/qrt-asset-allocation-forecasting)
- Asset allocation: LightGBM + SeqTabNet (1D-CNN + MLP) 60/40 rank blend gave **+0.0021** (t=+2.55, 11/12 folds). XGBoost as a third member was "not explored". — [hobbit-joufflu](https://github.com/hobbit-joufflu/Data-Challenge-2026)
- Grand Data Challenge 31st place kept LogisticRegression. Per-allocation models and blending were less performant. — [iliasbendali](https://github.com/iliasbendali/QRT-Grand-Data-Challenge)
- Asset allocation benchmark hyperparameters: LightGBM `max_depth=3`, `lr=0.01`, 500 rounds; Ridge α=1. — [lflsphere](https://github.com/lflsphere/QRT-Asset-Allocation-Perf)
- Electricity: ridge alone was best under the raw features. After adding time features, +LightGBM gave 0.558 → 0.569 and +pooled CatBoost 0.572. LightGBM used `num_leaves=4`. — [ryan-racer](https://github.com/ryan-racer/QRT-Electricity-Forecasting)
- Football: stacking of LightGBM + XGBoost + CatBoost gave 0.4913 online. A 50% TabICL + 50% CatBoost blend gave 0.4880, and a greedy ensemble 0.4873, all within LB noise. The 4th-place solution stacked XGBoost/CatBoost/TabNet. The 1st-public team ensembled SVM + XGB + CatBoost + MLP. — [louisrodembourg](https://github.com/louisrodembourg/qrt-football-prediction); [hugopuybareau](https://github.com/hugopuybareau/QRT_DATA_CHALLENGE); [ayoub-mg](https://github.com/ayoub-mg/QRT-Football-Challenge)
- AML: rank-average of 10 survival members 0.7238 vs best single (multi-horizon IPCW XGBoost) 0.7226, so +0.0012. It beat the best member on 11/15 folds and was more robust in the worst fold (0.690 vs 0.689). — [lucaspaulien](https://github.com/lucaspaulien/qrt-leukemia-survival)
- AML: stacking with a CoxPH meta-model scored 0.7541 vs the best single model (component-wise GB) at 0.7493, +0.0048 on submission scores. DeepSurv did not beat CoxPH/RSF (0.7113 vs 0.7160). — [coquetl](https://github.com/coquetl/Myeloid-Leukemia-Prediction); [MaxCoppa](https://github.com/MaxCoppa/Myeloid-Leukemia-Survival-Prediction)
- 2020 stock: the benchmark and top-15% solutions used shallow Random Forests (500 trees, depth 8) or LightGBM-RF. — [Challenge 23](https://challengedata.ens.fr/challenges/23); [lbrilh](https://github.com/lbrilh/QRT-StockReturnPrediction)
- Learning factors 2022: the model is fixed as linear. Top approaches were constrained optimisation in torch (exp-map, penalty, Lagrangian), all converging to about 0.149 training metric. — [mrmrob003](https://github.com/mrmrob003/qrt-data-challenge-2022)

### Inferences
- (Inference) A reliable 4-hour recipe: (1) LightGBM, shallow (`num_leaves` 4–31, `max_depth` 3–6, `learning_rate` 0.01–0.05, `min_child_samples` ≥ 20–100, `subsample` 0.8, `colsample_bytree` 0.7, early stopping on grouped folds); (2) ridge/logistic on standardised, cross-sectionally ranked features; (3) CatBoost if there are categoricals. Average rank-normalised predictions with equal weights. Skip neural networks and heavy stacking unless there is time left.
- (Inference) For multi-class football-style tasks, a goal-difference regressor (or its prediction as a feature) plus a classifier, with threshold tuning on draw probability, is what top entries converged on. Draws are the hardest class.

### Gaps
- No source compared LightGBM vs CatBoost vs XGBoost head-to-head on the 2020 stock data with grouped CV.
- I found no evidence on whether the official winners used ensembles.

## Q5. What do write-ups from QRT's in-person university hackathons say about approach and time management?

### Takeaway
QRT runs two very different in-person formats. **Data challenges** are tabular ML on a QRT dataset, scored by accuracy, Sharpe or rank metrics (QRT × TBF 2024: 5 h; IIT Delhi 2025; Grand Data Challenge Paris Oct 2025: 8 days). **"C3 — Code. Compute. Compete."** coding nights in 2026 are 2-hour HackerRank sets of 4–5 approximate-optimisation problems with partial credit, where teams running several AI coding agents in parallel placed top. Winners' common habits: a valid baseline submission very early, one owner per problem or track, a local scorer that mirrors the official metric, and a hard stop on new ideas in the final 10–20 minutes.

### Cited Findings
- **QRT × Télécom Business Finance hackathon, 6 June 2024:** "Code originally developed during a 5-hour hackathon by a team of three". Football outcome prediction. Code: concatenated HOME_/AWAY_ team statistics; target recoded to HOME_WINS − AWAY_WINS; dropped features with |corr| > 0.8; added win-rate features; nested `train_test_split` for train/val/test; LightGBM multiclass with `RandomizedSearchCV` over `num_leaves ∈ {15,31,63}`, `max_depth ∈ {5,6}`, `learning_rate=0.025`, `bagging_freq ∈ {5,6}`, `early_stopping_rounds=100`. — [rwitt74/QRT_hackathon (repo date 2024-09-26)](https://github.com/rwitt74/QRT_hackathon)
- A QRT × TBF participant team placed 1st public / 2nd private (0.4861 vs 0.4875) with an SVM + XGB + CatBoost + MLP ensemble. That this is the same 6 June 2024 event is an inference from the shared "QRT × TBF" name and dataset. — [ayoub-mg](https://github.com/ayoub-mg/QRT-Football-Challenge)
- **QRT hackathon at IIT Delhi, 2 March 2025:** analyse historical returns plus about 20 unknown daily features and "formulate an alpha, optimising for sharpe". The 5th of 190+ entrants used a simple formula: `alpha = Σ_features feature * corr(feature, returns) / exp(corr(feature, std(returns)))`. Steps: clean, drop unrecoverable entries, impute, compute each feature's correlation with returns and with return volatility. — [manogyasingh/qrt-hackathon (repo date 2025-03-25)](https://github.com/manogyasingh/qrt-hackathon)
