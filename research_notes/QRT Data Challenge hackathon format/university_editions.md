# QRT on-campus Data Challenge / hackathon editions at universities: format, rules, and the ETH Zürich 24 Nov 2026 event

Research date: 23 Nov 2026. "Confirmed" means stated on a cited page. Most details of past editions come from participants' GitHub repos, not from official QRT pages. QRT's own site has no public events page: qube-rt.com/events, /campus and /students return 404, and /careers gives only campus@qube-rt.com.

Two separate QRT campus formats turn up in 2024–2026. They need to be kept apart:
- **Format A, "Data Challenge" hackathons.** Short, in-person, data-science prediction tasks in teams. Seen in Paris (2024) and in France's "Grand Data Challenge" (2025). Problems often reuse or resemble the ENS Challenge Data datasets.
- **Format B, the "C3: Code, Compute, Compete" coding hackathon.** UK universities, autumn 2026. It is a 2-hour HackerRank optimisation contest run with OpenAI, where AI coding agents are explicitly part of the game.

The ETH event is titled "Data Challenge Hackathon". No public source says which format it uses (see Q6).

## Q2. What problem was set at each edition, and was it Kaggle-style prediction with a leaderboard?

### Takeaway
- Format A events are tabular ML prediction tasks with a hidden test set and accuracy-type metrics: football match outcome (2024), shorting/asset-allocation sign prediction on synthetic portfolio data (2025–26), and alpha construction scored on Sharpe (IIT Delhi 2025).
- Format B (C3, 2026) is not ML at all. It is four heuristic optimisation/puzzle problems plus a bonus, auto-graded on HackerRank with partial credit for answer quality.

### Cited Findings
**Paris, 6 Jun 2024 (Format A)**
- Football "Can you guess the winner?".
- The notebook loads `Train_Data/train_home_team_statistics_df.csv`, `train_away_team_statistics_df.csv` and `Y_train_1rknArQ.csv` (HOME_WINS/AWAY_WINS). It uses LightGBM multiclass and `accuracy_score`. — [rwitt74/QRT_hackathon](https://github.com/rwitt74/QRT_hackathon)
- These are the same files as QRT's ENS Challenge Data football challenge, which started 10 Jan 2024 with data from Sportmonks. — [challengedata.ens.fr/challenges/143](https://challengedata.ens.fr/challenges/143)
- A second participant describes the task as "Guessing the winner of soccer matches, using a large scale dataset of football metrics". — [iljay/QRT_DataChallenge](https://github.com/iljay/QRT_DataChallenge)

**Grand Data Challenge 2025 (France, 8 days)**
- Task: "prédire s'il fallait short des actifs d'un portfolio à partir de données synthétiques" (a classification task).
- Metric: accuracy. The target was relatively balanced.
- The author used LogReg, RandomForest, XGB and LGBM, and placed 31st of about 200. — [iliasbendali/QRT-Grand-Data-Challenge](https://github.com/iliasbendali/QRT-Grand-Data-Challenge)

**"QRT hackathon", March 2026, university unknown**
- Tabular classification. Files: `X_train.csv`, `X_test.csv`, `y_train.csv` with `ROW_ID`, `ALLOCATION`, `TS`, `RET_1`–`RET_20` and a binary `target`. Output is `submission.csv`, and the team validated with accuracy at a 0.5 threshold. — [ferdinandfd/QRT-Hackathon](https://github.com/ferdinandfd/QRT-Hackathon)
- This matches QRT's current ENS challenge, "Asset Allocation Performance Forecasting": predict whether an allocation's next-day return is positive, scored on accuracy, running since 22 Jan 2026. — [QuantVault listing](https://quantvault.org/quant-trading-competitions-2026.html); [Institut Louis Bachelier](https://www.institutlouisbachelier.org/en/on-the-program/actualites/data-challenge-a-2025-season-extended-to-2026-and-a-new-challenge-signed-by-qrt)

**IIT Delhi, 2 Mar 2025**
- "analyse historical returns data and values of ~20 different unknown features' daily data, and use it to formulate an alpha, optimising for sharpe."
- The author placed 5th of 190+ with a correlation-weighted feature sum. — [manogyasingh/qrt-hackathon](https://github.com/manogyasingh/qrt-hackathon)

**APAC Data Challenge 2025**
- Reconstruct liquid-asset performance (direction) from illiquid-asset returns. This is a classification task; the author selected models by F1-macro, which may not be the official metric. — [lhurr/qrt-data-challenge](https://github.com/lhurr/qrt-data-challenge)

**Imperial Algothon 2023 (QRT challenge)**
- Explain daily electricity-futures price changes in France and Germany from same-day weather, commodity and consumption data. It was explicitly "not a prediction problem".
- Metric: Spearman correlation. — [aa6dcc/QRT-Electricity-Pricing-Hackathon](https://github.com/aa6dcc/QRT-Electricity-Pricing-Hackathon)

**C3 at Imperial, 26 Oct 2026 (Format B)**
- Five problems: square packing (score = (L/A)^20, "very harsh"), word-search grid generation, a noisy black-box function, Masyu (shortest loop, runtime penalty after 1 s, 10 s timeout), and a bonus that "reassembled shredded text".
- This comes secondhand from an Edinburgh participant's prep brief. — [iamagoodacc/qrt-hackathon-2026, docs/C3_SETUP_BRIEF.md](https://github.com/iamagoodacc/qrt-hackathon-2026)

**C3 at Edinburgh, 16 Nov 2026**
- Problems: Word Search Generation, Square Packing++, Masyu, Black box, and Bonus: Document Lego. — [sbharga/qrt-c3](https://github.com/sbharga/qrt-c3); [okmissty/QRT-Hackathon](https://github.com/okmissty/QRT-Hackathon)
- "All five questions are **Approximate Solution** problems." — [CooperMcCloskey/QRT-Hackathon](https://github.com/CooperMcCloskey/QRT-Hackathon)
- The challenges were "a mix of optimisation, logic, search, and heuristic reconstruction problems designed by QRT with help from an LLM". — [okmissty/QRT-Hackathon](https://github.com/okmissty/QRT-Hackathon)

### Inferences
- The Edinburgh problem set is essentially the same as Imperial's, even though the Edinburgh prep brief expected "the problems will be new". So QRT appears to reuse one C3 problem set across campuses within a season.
- The Paris format reused an ENS Challenge Data dataset. An ETH "Data Challenge" could similarly reuse a current or past ENS QRT dataset, such as asset-allocation sign prediction. This is unconfirmed.

### Gaps
- No problem statement is public for the ETH edition, the Oxford/Cambridge/Warwick C3 rounds, or the DurHack 2025 QRT track.
- It is unclear whether the 2024 Paris event showed a live leaderboard.

## Q3. What platform hosted submissions, and how many submissions per team?

### Takeaway
- C3 runs on HackerRank, with one source file per problem pasted into an online judge and run on hidden tests.
- For Format A on-campus events, no platform is documented. Participants produced `submission.csv` files against hidden test sets, consistent with a Kaggle-style or ENS-style upload.
- Submission limits are not documented anywhere I found.

### Cited Findings
- C3 Imperial: "A 2-hour timed test on **HackerRank**: four optimisation problems plus one bonus… Sample tests are not scored." — [iamagoodacc/qrt-hackathon-2026, docs/C3_SETUP_BRIEF.md](https://github.com/iamagoodacc/qrt-hackathon-2026)
- C3 Edinburgh: "The files keep the HackerRank-style wrappers used during the competition. Python solutions write to `OUTPUT_PATH` when run through the platform." — [okmissty/QRT-Hackathon](https://github.com/okmissty/QRT-Hackathon)
- C3 Edinburgh: "Solutions are pasted into an online judge as **one source file** and run on **hidden tests**."
  - The same team recorded judge scores per submission.
  - For the Black box problem, the judge gave noisy feedback while the "official noiseless score" counted at the end. — [CooperMcCloskey/QRT-Hackathon](https://github.com/CooperMcCloskey/QRT-Hackathon) (README.md and AGENTS.md)
- Edinburgh teams' pre-event questions for organisers included "Does the judge count the best submission or the last one?", "Is there a limit on submissions?" and "Does one person submit for the whole team?". This shows these rules were not published in advance. — [CooperMcCloskey AGENTS.md](https://github.com/CooperMcCloskey/QRT-Hackathon); [iamagoodacc TEAM.md](https://github.com/iamagoodacc/qrt-hackathon-2026)
- Format A (2026 hackathon): the pipeline writes `submission.csv` from the supplied `X_test.csv`. — [ferdinandfd/QRT-Hackathon](https://github.com/ferdinandfd/QRT-Hackathon)

### Inferences
- The Edinburgh FEEDBACK.md logs several judge runs per problem within minutes, which suggests multiple submissions per problem were allowed at C3.
- A data-style ETH event would most likely take CSV prediction uploads to a scoring page with a leaderboard. No source confirms the platform (Kaggle in-class, ENS, custom QRT or Codalab).

### Gaps
- No source names the platform for the Paris 2024, Grand Data Challenge 2025 or ETH events.
- No documented submission cap for any edition.
- No evidence of CrunchDAO or Codalab use for campus events.

## Q4. Metric, public vs private leaderboard, tie-breakers, judging of presentations/code

### Takeaway
- Format A: accuracy for classification tasks (football, asset allocation, shorting), Sharpe for the IIT Delhi alpha task, and Spearman correlation for the Algothon electricity task.
- C3: partial credit per hidden test, based on answer quality, summed to points. Imperial's maximum was 4300.
- Presentations were judged at Imperial's Algothon, but nothing indicates presentations at C3 or at the short Data Challenge events.
- I found no documented public/private leaderboard split or tie-breaker for any campus edition.

### Cited Findings
- C3 scoring:
  - "Partial credit: each hidden test pays a share of its points based on answer *quality*… An invalid answer scores 0."
  - The bonus "only counts if the team reaches 70% on every main problem."
  - Imperial results: winner 3988/4300 (93%), average team 2301/4300 (54%).
  - Source: [iamagoodacc/qrt-hackathon-2026, docs/C3_SETUP_BRIEF.md](https://github.com/iamagoodacc/qrt-hackathon-2026)
- Imperial winner: 3987.89 of 4300 points (per the search index of the now-404 repo). — [xRyann2255/qrt-hackathon-2026](https://github.com/xRyann2255/qrt-hackathon-2026)
- Edinburgh bonus rule: "Problem 5… points only count if the final score is strictly greater than 70% on each of Problems 1, 2, 3 and 4. Exactly 70% does not qualify, and an average above 70% does not qualify." — [CooperMcCloskey/QRT-Hackathon](https://github.com/CooperMcCloskey/QRT-Hackathon)
- Format A metrics:
  - Accuracy (Grand Data Challenge 2025) — [iliasbendali](https://github.com/iliasbendali/QRT-Grand-Data-Challenge)
  - Accuracy used in the Paris 2024 football notebook — [rwitt74](https://github.com/rwitt74/QRT_hackathon)
  - Sharpe (IIT Delhi) — [manogyasingh](https://github.com/manogyasingh/qrt-hackathon)
  - Spearman (Algothon 2023 QRT) — [aa6dcc](https://github.com/aa6dcc/QRT-Electricity-Pricing-Hackathon)
- Presentations: the Imperial Algothon 2023 had a final presentation ("we messed up the final presentation"), even though that team "got the highest score" on the QRT challenge. — [MorganREN/Algothon_IC_2023](https://github.com/MorganREN/Algothon_IC_2023)
- C3 Edinburgh schedule ends with "20:30-21:00 results, prizes and networking with QRT". No presentations are listed. — [UK Hackathons](https://ukhackathons.com/events/qrt-x-compsoc-c3-coding-hackathon)

### Inferences
- If the ETH event follows Format A on an ENS-like dataset, expect accuracy (binary or 3-class) on a hidden test set. Overfitting a public split would be the main risk.
- The Algothon example shows that in some multi-sponsor formats, leaderboard score alone does not decide winners.

### Gaps
- No source confirms a public/private split, tie-breakers, or code/presentation judging for C3, Paris 2024 or ETH.

## Q5. Team size, rules on AI tools, internet, pre-written code, external data

### Takeaway
- **Team size:**
  - C3: 3–4 (Edinburgh: up to 4, individuals welcome)
  - Paris 2024: 3 (one repo says 6)
  - Grand Data Challenge: apparently individual
  - ETH: registration is individual, and the page gives no team size
- **AI tools:** at C3, AI is not just allowed but sponsored. Each participant gets $25 of OpenAI credits, the problems were designed with an LLM, and the Imperial winner orchestrated 22 AI coding-agent sessions.
- **Internet, pre-written code and external data:** I found no published rules for any edition. Participants listed them as open questions to ask at the briefing.

### Inferences
- Participants' tools ran on their own laptops: Python and standard ML libraries (pandas, scikit-learn, LightGBM, XGBoost) in Format A repos, and Python/C++ against a judge in C3. No event-provided compute was mentioned anywhere.
- Given QRT's 2026 OpenAI partnership in the UK series, AI assistants are unlikely to be banned at a late-2026 QRT event. That is not confirmed for ETH, and the ETH page mentions no OpenAI credits.
- ETH registrants sign up individually, so teams may be formed or assigned on the night. This is unconfirmed.

### Gaps
- No published rulebook for any edition covering internet access, external data, pre-written code or LLM use.
- No information on whether the ETH event allows or provides AI credits.

## Q8. What did winners and participants say afterwards?

### Takeaway
Public reflections are almost all GitHub READMEs. No LinkedIn or Reddit posts were found through search. The main lessons:
- **C3:** orchestrate many AI agents in parallel. Build a local scorer first. Always hold a valid baseline, because invalid answers score 0. Then use greedy plus simulated annealing in C++. Watch the 70%-on-every-problem bonus gate.
- **Data format:** feature engineering on return windows and simple tree models. Blending and per-group models did not help in one case. Watch for target-encoding leakage in CV.

### Cited Findings
- **Imperial C3 winner** (solo, 1st of 68 teams / ~240 participants, 3987.89/4300) published a "write-up, solutions and interactive demos". The repo was unavailable (404) on 23 Nov 2026. — [xRyann2255/qrt-hackathon-2026](https://github.com/xRyann2255/qrt-hackathon-2026)
- **Lessons from the winner, as reported by an Edinburgh team's brief:**
  - "Several independent attempts at a problem, keeping the best, beat one bigger attempt."
  - Each agent followed a fixed protocol: restate the problem, verify answers, and only replace the saved best if the score improved.
  - Source: [iamagoodacc/qrt-hackathon-2026, docs/C3_SETUP_BRIEF.md](https://github.com/iamagoodacc/qrt-hackathon-2026)
- **Edinburgh team's live log:**
  - A second AI agent reviewed the first agent's work during the contest.
  - On the noisy black-box problem they reverse-engineered the scoring as reconstruction error and fitted Fourier coefficients.
  - With minutes left they had "Nothing… confirmed as submitted yet" and had to rush a submission. This is a cautionary example about submitting early.
  - Source: [CooperMcCloskey FEEDBACK.md](https://github.com/CooperMcCloskey/QRT-Hackathon)
- **Edinburgh team clock:**
  - Everyone skims every statement in the first 10 minutes.
  - Valid baselines by about 25–40 minutes.
  - Freeze 20 minutes before the end.
  - Confirm best submissions 5 minutes before the end.
  - Source: [CooperMcCloskey AGENTS.md](https://github.com/CooperMcCloskey/QRT-Hackathon)
- **Earlier C3 round:** 2nd of ~300. The repo contains `src/qrt_agent` and `.cursor/rules`, which indicates AI-agent tooling. — [a-rune/spinning-doughnut-qrt](https://github.com/a-rune/spinning-doughnut-qrt)
- **Grand Data Challenge 2025:** per-allocation models and blending were "moins performants ici" (less performant here) and were dropped. 31st of about 200. — [iliasbendali](https://github.com/iliasbendali/QRT-Grand-Data-Challenge)
- **2026 asset-allocation hackathon team:**
  - Features: EWM means and volatility, slope, autocorrelations and streaks over `RET_1`–`RET_20`, then ExtraTrees.
  - The team warns that "`TS` target encoding is generated before the model's cross-validation split, so its reported mean accuracy should not be treated as an unbiased held-out score."
  - They also checked train/test drift with the Wasserstein distance.
  - Source: [ferdinandfd/QRT-Hackathon](https://github.com/ferdinandfd/QRT-Hackathon)
- **IIT Delhi:** 5th of 190+ with a simple correlation-weighted alpha. — [manogyasingh](https://github.com/manogyasingh/qrt-hackathon)
- **Imperial Algothon 2023:** "My team got the highest score for challenge: Explain the price of electricity (from QRT). However, we messed up the final presentation." — [MorganREN](https://github.com/MorganREN/Algothon_IC_2023)

### Inferences
- For short QRT events, the repeated lessons are:
  - Get a valid scored submission in early.
  - Build a local validation or scoring harness that mirrors the official metric.
  - Avoid leakage. In time-indexed data (`TS` columns), random CV overstates scores.
  - Split work by problem or by person.
